The Construction and What Remains

The Construction and Artifacts of the Eiffel Tower

The Eiffel Tower itself is the most important physical object that remains from its construction and the 1889 World’s Fair. More than a century later, visitors can still see the enormous iron structure that engineers and workers created during the late nineteenth century. The tower was constructed mainly from puddled iron, a type of iron commonly used during that period. More than 18,000 individual iron parts were manufactured for the project. These pieces had to be produced with great accuracy because they would later be connected together to create the complete structure.

Millions of rivets were used during the construction process. Rivets are metal fasteners that were used to connect the different iron pieces. Teams of workers heated the rivets, placed them into holes in the iron, and secured them in position. This method created strong connections between the different parts of the tower.

The four large legs of the Eiffel Tower were another important engineering challenge. They had to be positioned extremely carefully so that they would meet correctly as the tower became narrower toward the top. During construction, engineers constantly measured and checked the structure.

Original drawings, plans, photographs, and documents related to the project have also survived. They help historians understand the engineering methods used to create such an ambitious structure in the nineteenth century. However, the greatest artifact is still the tower itself. Although it has been repaired, repainted, and modified many times throughout its history, much of its fundamental structure remains. Visitors today can stand underneath the same enormous iron framework that visitors to the 1889 World’s Fair saw. The Eiffel Tower therefore acts almost like a giant historical artifact. Instead of being displayed inside a museum, it continues to stand in its original location in Paris, connecting the modern city with an important moment in the history of engineering.
