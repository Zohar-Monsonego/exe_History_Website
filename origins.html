The Origins of the Eiffel Tower

The story of the Eiffel Tower began in the late nineteenth century, when France was preparing for the 1889 World’s Fair, also known as the Exposition Universelle. The exhibition was organized in Paris to celebrate the 100th anniversary of the French Revolution. France wanted to impress visitors from around the world and show its progress in science, technology, engineering, and industry.

As part of the preparations, a competition was organized to create an impressive structure that would stand at the entrance to the exhibition. The idea was to build a tower about 300 meters high. At the time, creating a structure of this height was an enormous engineering challenge. The first design for the tower was created by engineers Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, who worked for Gustave Eiffel’s engineering company. Their early design showed a large iron tower with four legs that joined together as they rose toward the sky. Architect Stephen Sauvestre later helped improve the appearance of the design and added decorative elements.

Gustave Eiffel became interested in the project and supported it. The design was eventually accepted, and Eiffel’s company received the responsibility for constructing the tower. Because of his important role in the project, the structure later became known as the Eiffel Tower. Construction began in January 1887. Building such a large iron structure required careful planning and great accuracy. Thousands of iron parts were manufactured separately and then assembled at the construction site. The workers had to make sure that every part was placed correctly so that the enormous structure would remain stable.

Completion of the Eiffel Tower

After a little more than two years of construction, the Eiffel Tower was completed in March 1889. It was ready in time for the World’s Fair, which opened in May. At approximately 300 meters tall, the tower was unlike anything people had seen before. It became the tallest structure in the world at the time and immediately attracted attention from visitors around the world.
