Who was involved in the constructions of the Eiffel Tower?

The people behind the Eiffel Tower

Many people were involved in the creation of the Eiffel Tower. Although the tower is named after Gustave Eiffel, the famous French engineer was not the only person responsible for its design and construction. Gustave Eiffel was born in Dijon, France, in 1832. He became a successful engineer and specialized in metal structures. Before the Eiffel Tower was built, his company had already worked on bridges, railway structures, and other major engineering projects. His experience with iron construction made him an important figure in the development of the tower.

However, the original idea for the tower came mainly from two engineers who worked for Eiffel’s company: Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier. In 1884, they developed an early plan for a tower about 300 meters high. Their design included four large iron supports that would come together as the tower became higher. At first, Gustave Eiffel was not completely enthusiastic about the design. The architect Stephen Sauvestre was asked to improve its appearance. He added architectural and decorative features that made the tower more attractive. After these changes, Eiffel became more interested in the project and strongly supported it. There were also hundreds of workers involved in producing and assembling the iron pieces used to construct the tower. Their work required great precision because thousands of different pieces had to fit together correctly.

Not everyone supported the project. A group of well-known French artists, writers, and architects strongly criticized the tower before it was completed. They believed that such a huge iron structure would be ugly and would damage the beauty and traditional appearance of Paris. In 1887, some of them published a famous protest against the tower. Despite the criticism, Eiffel continued to defend the project. He believed the tower demonstrated the possibilities of modern engineering and scientific progress. When it was finally completed in 1889, the tower attracted enormous attention. Over time, many people who had criticized it were overshadowed by its growing popularity. Today, Gustave Eiffel’s name remains permanently connected with one of the most recognizable structures in the world.
